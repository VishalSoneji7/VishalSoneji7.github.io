frustum_culling.log Prime Engine · C++

Finding the Cut: Culling Without Killing Fidelity

The obvious approach is cull as aggressively as possible for maximum performance — but push the distance too close and players notice objects popping in. The real work was finding the threshold where culling dropped draw calls significantly, but the boundary sat just outside what the player's eye naturally tracks. It's not a formula — it's iteration. The lesson: performance optimization is only good if the player never knows it happened.

physics_collision.log Prime Engine · C++

Collision From Scratch: Dot Products and Guesswork

Building physics from scratch in Prime Engine meant no rules — just vectors. Collision detection came down to dot and cross products to figure out not just whether objects had hit, but which face the impact came from and what direction to apply response force. Getting that wrong meant tunnelling or wrong-angle launches. The breakthrough: treat every collision as a conversation between two normals and let the math decide, not hardcoded responses.

animation_blend.log Prime Engine · C++

Blending Without Breaking: Keeping Animations Human

Naively blending two animations lets the skeleton happily twist a spine 180 degrees if the math allows it. My constraint: the character's line of motion must always read naturally, even mid-blend. That meant clamping rotation ranges on spine and shoulder bones, and locking scale at 1 throughout. If a blend makes a character look like they're fighting themselves, the player feels it immediately — even if they can't name why.
